# Style and template control

## Choose the active style mode

The user may select or combine these modes:

- **Template-led:** preserve an exact master, layouts, theme, and approved assets.
- **Reference-led:** derive stated qualities from selected decks, screenshots, brand
  guides, or mood references without claiming they are a formal master.
- **Art-directed:** implement the user's verbal direction for tone, typography,
  palette, composition, imagery, density, and motion.
- **Delegated:** propose a small number of coherent directions and let the user choose
  before extensive production.

Do not impose light, dark, minimal, corporate, editorial, playful, technical, or any
other house aesthetic globally. Accessibility, legibility, provenance, and
non-destructive work remain harness constraints; visual taste belongs to the brief.

## Inspect a template before use

Record:

- template or approved-deck source;
- slide aspect ratio;
- master and layout names used by representative slide roles;
- theme fonts, color roles, background behavior, and title geometry;
- margins, guides, footer, page-number, logo, and safe-area behavior;
- placeholder behavior and any slide-level overrides;
- approved logos, icons, photography, fonts, and asset restrictions.

Treat the current live editor/master as stronger template evidence than screenshots
or remembered values. If the brief requires an exact template and it is unavailable,
stop styled production and report the exact missing input. A provisional unstyled
storyboard is acceptable when content evidence is sufficient.

## Build a coherent system

Translate the selected direction into explicit roles before styling slides:

- display, title, subtitle, body, annotation, and code/data typography;
- primary, secondary, neutral, state, and emphasis colors;
- spacing rhythm, safe margins, grid, and alignment behavior;
- cover, section, content, data, visual, and closing slide families;
- image crop/padding rules, icon family, connector language, and chart treatment;
- intentional exceptions such as cover scale, closing composition, or full-bleed art.

Consistency means shared roles behave predictably; it does not require every slide to
look identical. Preserve deliberate variation that supports the story.

## Preserve and change deliberately

- Work from a copy of the selected template-bearing deck.
- Use native layouts and placeholders when they serve the brief.
- Reapply or reset layouts only when the target mapping is known and inspect the
  result immediately.
- Do not rebuild a supplied master as slide-level backgrounds merely for convenience.
- Do not globally normalize titles, colors, or positions until deliberate exceptions
  are identified.
- When the user changes direction, update shared style roles first, then inspect all
  consumers and repair only the invalidated exceptions.
- Never claim reference-led or art-directed work is official brand compliance without
  an approved brand source.

## Editability choices

Keep titles, subtitles, body text, annotations, values, tables, and likely review
changes native by default. Prefer native shapes, charts, and connectors when they can
meet the selected design. Flatten only where the chosen asset or delivery constraint
justifies it, and preserve the editable source or regeneration path.
