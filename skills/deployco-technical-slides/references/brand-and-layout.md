# Brand, master, and layout preservation

## Source of truth

Use the actual DeployCo Slide Template Master or an explicitly approved DeployCo
deck containing that master. Do not recreate the theme from memory, sample a brand
from a screenshot, synthesize custom backgrounds, or substitute Tomoro colors.

Before editing, record:

- template/deck path or shared-deck URL;
- master and layout names used by cover, content, section, and closing slides;
- slide aspect ratio;
- theme font families and the size/style of representative content titles;
- theme colors sampled from the master, including charcoal, cyan, mint, lavender,
  neutral backgrounds, rules, and accessible text colors;
- approved logos, icons, and any asset restrictions.

If this provenance cannot be established, stop. Report the missing template or
asset, the slides affected, and the exact input needed to continue.

## Visual register

- Favor a light or white canvas and native DeployCo backgrounds/layouts.
- Use the template’s charcoal for primary text and structural lines. Use cyan, mint,
  and lavender accents only as sampled from the current template; never treat
  remembered hex values as authority.
- Use the modern sans-serif family defined by the theme. Use monospace sparingly for
  exact commands, contracts, paths, or code-shaped evidence.
- Keep one consistent title size, position, and wrapping behavior across content
  slides. Derive it from a correct template/example slide. Allow explicit cover and
  closing exceptions; do not let diagram density create accidental exceptions.
- Preserve the master’s margins, guides, footer behavior, page numbering, and logo
  clear space. Keep important content inside a safe inset even when the slide editor
  permits edge-to-edge placement.
- Prefer thin rules and precise connectors. Avoid gradients, shadows, glow, bevels,
  3D effects, decorative illustration, and cartoon styling.

## Editability contract

Keep these elements native and editable:

- titles, subtitles, body text, annotations, callouts, and footers;
- simple cards, dividers, status chips, arrows, connectors, and basic process nodes;
- values, labels, and takeaways likely to change during review.

Use raster assets only for diagrams or illustrations that materially benefit from
image generation or would be impractical to build cleanly with native shapes. Do not
flatten a whole slide or bake narrative text into a background image.

## Density and hierarchy

- Give each slide one conclusion expressed by its title.
- Use the subtitle for the governing reason or boundary, not a second headline.
- Let the primary visual carry the system relationship; use compact annotations for
  exact constraints, evidence, and tradeoffs.
- Do not remove technical detail solely to create empty space. First tighten labels,
  turn prose into a structured comparison, move implementation detail to a paired
  slide, or layer depth into speaker notes while preserving the visible conclusion.
- Check all text and diagrams at slideshow scale, not only while zoomed into the
  editor.

## Preservation rules

- Work on a copy and keep the known-good source untouched.
- Do not replace the master, relink every slide to a generic layout, or globally
  reset slides without a before/after visual review.
- Do not overwrite proven diagram files. Add a versioned successor and update one
  centralized source mapping.
- Do not stretch images. Use proportional crop or white padding to fit the allocated
  region.
- When import/export changes the master or theme, return to the approved template and
  reapply the correct layout rather than compensating with custom backgrounds.
